How Electrical Customers Search

A dead outlet and a panel upgrade are different customers

One wants someone today. The other is collecting quotes for a job that may need a permit and an inspection. The site should let each one find their path without digging through the other's.

Emergency calls, handled honestly

Click-to-call on every mobile screen. If you take after-hours calls, say so. If you do not, say when you open. Pages about hazards should tell the reader to get clear and call 911 first when anyone is hurt, there is fire or smoke, or a line is down, and to call the utility after that.

One page per job type

Panel upgrades, EV charger installs, generator installs, rewiring, lighting, troubleshooting. Each page covers one job, what the visit involves, and how to book it. Leave off anything you do not do.

Quote requests for planned work

Larger jobs start with an estimate. A short form that asks for the job type and town gives you a better first conversation than an empty message box. Say how quoting works and whether there is a service-call fee.

Service-area pages only where you work

Town pages follow the same rule as job pages: only where you actually work, and only with something specific to say about the work there. Google's spam policies name city pages that funnel visitors to one page as doorway abuse. A handful of real pages is worth more than fifty with the town name swapped.

License and insurance where customers look

Someone planning a job that may need a permit and an inspection wants to know the electrician is licensed before asking for a quote. Where your state or town issues a license, show the number in the footer and on the contact page, with a plain statement that you carry insurance. Requirements differ from state to state; our guide on license numbers on contractor websites covers what to check.

Photos of your own work

Your panels, your installs, your crew. A clean panel photo says more about your standards than any adjective on the page.

Google's Written Rules

Google's rules for an electrician's profile, reviews and ads

An electrician can show up in the map listing, in the regular search results and, if you pay for them, in Local Services Ads. Each runs on written policies. Your site and your profile should say the same thing about your business, in the way Google allows.

Business Profile as a service-area business

If customers do not come to your shop, Google's guidelines say to hide your address and set a service area no more than about two hours of driving from your base. Use your real business name on the profile, without added keywords. Source: Google Business Profile guidelines.

Reviews you ask for, never pay for

Asking every customer for an honest review is allowed. Paying for one, or offering a discount or free service in return, is not. Neither is asking only the happy customers or discouraging negative reviews. Source: Google Maps content policy.

Local Services Ads readiness

Readiness comes down to licenses, insurance, background checks, reviews and business information that matches everywhere it appears. Google treats electrical work as an urgent category and screens electricians before the ads run, including background checks on service professionals, with other checks that depend on your business type and state, and it says missed calls may count against your responsiveness. Sources: screening requirements, how ads are ranked. Our guide on Local Services Ads for contractors has the details.

Common Questions

Straight answers for electrical contractors

Which electrical jobs deserve their own page?

The ones you actually do and want more of. For one shop that is panel upgrades and EV chargers. For another it is service calls and generators. The list should come from your own job history, not a template. Each page covers one job, what the visit involves, and how to book it.

Should our site list prices?

That is your call. Quoting after seeing the job is reasonable. What helps is explaining how quoting works: whether there is a service-call fee, whether estimates are free, and when after-hours rates apply. A clear process earns trust even without a price list.

Where does our license number belong on the site?

In the footer of every page and on the contact page, if your state or town issues one. Whether it is required in advertising varies by state, so check with your licensing board. Our guide on contractor license numbers walks through what to look for.

Are we ready for Google Local Services Ads?

Readiness means your license, insurance, background checks and business details can pass Google's screening and match what your website and Business Profile say. Google also says missed calls may hurt your responsiveness in those ads. We build the site and profile so the details agree. Running the ads is paid advertising, quoted separately.

No. Our work so far has been in healthcare and early education, and electrical contractors would be new for us. We would rather say so than show you a portfolio we do not have. Local search, Business Profile setup and call tracking are the same discipline in any trade. Which jobs you want more of, and how you quote them, has to come from you.
