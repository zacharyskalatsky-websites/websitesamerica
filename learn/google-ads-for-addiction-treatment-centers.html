Google Ads for Addiction Treatment Centers: What the Policies Require

By Websites America · October 5, 2026 · 9 min read

An addiction treatment center can run Google Ads in the United States and Canada, but only after two certifications: LegitScript's, then Google's. The restrictions do not stop there. The audience tools are limited, and the conversion tracking most other industries install by default can disclose patient information. This guide works from what Google, Microsoft and LegitScript publish, and says so where we could not find a published rule.

This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm your situation with the platforms and with counsel.

Can an addiction treatment center advertise on Google?

Google's healthcare and medicines policy says U.S. advertisers of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services must be certified as an addiction services provider by the LegitScript Certification program. It then adds that advertisers must also be certified with Google. Canada has the same two steps. Google announced on February 3, 2025 that it would allow these ads in Canada.

Google's addiction services policy page also lists Australia, France, Ireland and New Zealand. In those four countries the main policy limits these ads to government entities.

The scope is wide. The policy covers clinical treatment providers, sober living environments, lead generators and referral agencies, and crisis hotlines. Google's main policy is plain about the result: services not eligible for LegitScript certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services on Google. LegitScript generally does not certify lead generators or non-clinical sober homes, as we explain in our guide to LegitScript certification for addiction treatment websites.

Services for impulse control disorders, behavioral addictions such as gambling, and nicotine addiction fall outside this policy.

How do you get certified with Google?

LegitScript comes first. Its fees, standards and timeline are covered in our LegitScript guide. Once approved, you apply to Google through its healthcare-related advertising application and select "Addiction Services Provider." The form asks for the country where you hold the license and which permissions you want. One of them is "Target restricted keywords related to drug and alcohol addiction." The addiction services policy says to submit a separate application for each location or group of locations where you want to advertise.

What can your ads and landing pages say?

We found no published Google list of the addiction keywords that permission covers. The policy restricts the promotion of the services themselves, so do not plan a campaign around a word list.

The text rules come from Google's Misrepresentation policy. Its "unacceptable business practices" section prohibits impersonating other businesses in your ads or on your website, and making it seem you are supported by another brand, organization or government entity when you are not. LegitScript's certification standards add that you must identify yourself clearly in your ads and not use third-party business names in them.

The landing page has two sets of rules. Google's destination requirements require the display URL domain to match the final URL, the page to work, and the page to be crawlable by Google's ad review bot. Microsoft's healthcare policy goes further and says landing pages and any URLs used in the ad must be certified. Google's healthcare and medicines policy ties eligibility to the domain in general terms: for most healthcare policies, an ad can run in allowed locations when "the domain is properly certified." LegitScript requires you to list every domain you own or control, so the safe course on either platform is to send paid traffic only to a domain that appears on your LegitScript application. A new campaign microsite is one more domain to disclose.

Can you use remarketing or Customer Match?

No. Google's health in personalized advertising policy does not allow advertiser-curated audiences, Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion or lookalike segments when you promote health services. "Your data" is Google's current name for remarketing. The policy's examples include counseling services for addiction, naming alcohol addiction.

Custom segments that use sensitive ads or landing pages serve only on Display, to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. Users under 18 receive no personalized ads at all.

In practice, a rehab cannot show ads to someone because that person visited its site last week. Search intent, location, keywords and Google's predefined audiences, which exclude sensitive signals, have to do the work that remarketing does in other industries.

Why is conversion tracking the hard part?

Because a typical Google setup places tags on the pages where people ask for addiction care, and Google says it does not offer a business associate agreement (BAA) for Google Analytics. We found no Google statement offering one for Google Ads tags either.

Google's HIPAA guidance for Google Analytics says Google does not offer BAAs for the service and that HIPAA-regulated customers may only use it on pages that are not HIPAA-covered. It warns that unauthenticated pages related to the provision of health care are more likely to be covered.

HHS's online tracking guidance says a tracking vendor that receives protected health information (PHI) is a business associate and a BAA is required. Its own example is a person booking an appointment through a clinic website. A June 2024 court ruling vacated one narrow part of that guidance. The parts that matter for intake forms remain. Our article on HIPAA, tracking pixels and behavioral health websites covers the ruling and the leaks in detail.

Google Ads conversion tracking has its own limit. Google's offline conversion help recommends enhanced conversions for leads, which adds email addresses and phone numbers to imported conversions. Google's own customer data policy says conversions related to sensitive categories, including health or medical information such as purchases of medical services, cannot be used for measurement in enhanced conversions. So the setup Google recommends to most advertisers is one its policy closes to a treatment program.

That leaves two approaches. The lower-risk one is to count inquiries and admissions in your own systems, by campaign, and compare them to spend. Google's bidding gets less signal, but no conversion data leaves the program.

The other is a standard offline import, which sends Google a click ID, a conversion name and a time, with no name or email. It still tells Google that a specific ad click became an inquiry at an addiction program. Whether that is PHI in your setup is a question for a healthcare privacy attorney to answer before anyone turns the import on.

Whatever you choose, never send names, emails, phone numbers, insurance details or diagnoses to Google, and never name a conversion after a level of care.

Are call ads and call tracking safe?

Not by default. Google's call reporting uses forwarding numbers and records call duration, start time, whether the call connected, and the caller's phone number for calls over 15 seconds. A phone number tied to a call to a rehab is sensitive information. Decide with counsel whether call reporting belongs in your account.

Third-party call tracking raises the same question with more data. Recordings, transcripts and call scoring can all hold PHI. A vendor that holds PHI for you needs a signed BAA, and a vendor that will not sign should not get it. If a tracking number reaches anyone but your own admissions staff, ask LegitScript about it before you apply.

Does Microsoft Advertising require LegitScript too?

Yes, and its rules are narrower than Google's. Microsoft's healthcare and pharmaceutical policy allows drug and alcohol addiction recovery advertising only in the United States. It requires LegitScript certification for rehab facilities, group therapy, sober living, halfway houses and similar services. Landing pages and ad URLs must be certified, and lead generation ads are not allowed.

What will get your account suspended?

A violation of the addiction services policy itself brings a warning first. Google's addiction services policy says it will issue a warning at least seven days before any suspension.

Some violations bring no warning at all. Google treats unacceptable business practices under its Misrepresentation policy as egregious. It treats circumventing systems, part of its abusing the ad network policy, the same way. Google's circumventing examples include opening new accounts after a suspension and showing its reviewers different content than users see. For both, Google says accounts are suspended upon detection and without prior warning, and the advertiser is not allowed to advertise with Google Ads again. Reinstatement happens only in compelling circumstances.

In this industry, examples that fit those categories include a second account opened after a suspension, a landing page that shows Google's reviewers something different from what patients see, and ads written in another program's name. LegitScript has a rule of its own here. Its terms presume that continuing to advertise on a platform that requires certification after you apply is an attempt to circumvent the process.

How much should you budget?

We do not publish cost-per-click figures for addiction keywords. We could not find any from Google or another primary source, and the numbers that circulate online come from agencies and tool vendors. Use the estimates inside your own Google Ads account after certification, and judge the result by admissions in your own records.

What belongs on a pre-launch checklist?

  • LegitScript certification approved, with every domain you own listed on the application
  • Google addiction services certification approved for each location or location group you target, with the restricted addiction keyword permission requested
  • Ads point only to a certified domain, with display and final URL domains matching
  • Ad copy names your program, with no other program's name and no implied affiliations
  • Landing page matches your license: services, levels of care, addresses and real photos
  • No remarketing, Customer Match, lookalike or audience expansion in any campaign
  • Google Analytics and ad tags kept off intake, insurance, booking and thank-you pages
  • Enhanced conversions turned off, and no names, emails or phone numbers sent to Google
  • Conversion names that describe an action, not a level of care or diagnosis
  • A signed BAA with every call tracking, chat and form vendor that touches PHI
  • Every tracking number rings your own admissions staff
  • Admissions counted by campaign in your own systems
  • Counsel review of the tracking setup before the first dollar is spent

Organic search avoids most of these rules and builds over time. We cover it in SEO for addiction treatment centers.

Common questions about Google Ads for addiction treatment

Can a rehab advertise on Google Ads?

Yes, in the United States and Canada, if it holds LegitScript addiction treatment certification and is then also certified with Google. Services that are not eligible for LegitScript certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services on Google.

Can I use remarketing for addiction treatment ads?

No. Google's health in personalized advertising policy does not allow advertiser-curated audiences, Customer Match, your data segments (formerly remarketing), audience expansion or lookalike segments for ads in this category. Its examples include counseling for addiction, including alcohol addiction.

Can I use Google Analytics or enhanced conversions on a treatment center website?

Only with great care. Google says it does not offer business associate agreements for Google Analytics and that HIPAA-regulated customers may only use it on pages that are not HIPAA-covered. Google's customer data policy says conversions related to health or medical information cannot be used for measurement in enhanced conversions.

Does Microsoft Advertising require LegitScript certification for addiction treatment?

Yes. Microsoft allows drug and alcohol addiction recovery ads only in the United States, requires LegitScript certification, requires landing pages and any URLs used in the ad to be certified, and does not allow lead generation ads.

What gets a treatment center's Google Ads account suspended without warning?

Google treats unacceptable business practices, such as impersonating another business or implying support from an organization you are not affiliated with, and circumventing its systems as egregious violations. Accounts are suspended upon detection, without prior warning, and the advertiser is not allowed to advertise with Google Ads again.
