Google Local Services Ads for Plumbers, Electricians and HVAC Contractors

By Websites America · October 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Plenty of advice about Local Services Ads is out of date. Some of it still tells contractors to chase the green "Google Guaranteed" check mark, a badge Google has since replaced with a single one called Google Verified. In 2026 Google also began moving trade advertisers into Google Ads itself.

The rules below come from Google's own help pages as of October 2026. Google changes this program often, so check your own dashboard before you act on any of it.

What are Google Local Services Ads, and where do they show?

They are pay-per-lead ads for local service businesses, shown prominently in Google Search results and in Google Maps when someone looks for a service you offer in an area you chose.

Google's getting started page says the unit "is shown prominently in Google Search results when people search for the services you offer in areas you've chosen." The ad previews your profile, with name, hours, rating, reviews, photos and badge, according to Google's ad overview. The phone number on that ad is a Google tracking number that forwards to your line.

Plumbing, electrical and HVAC are all eligible. Google's migration notice names all three among the select U.S. home and storefront service advertisers in the first phase of a move, which began in August 2026, into a pay-per-lead campaign inside Google Ads. Service-area businesses without a storefront are scheduled for a later phase in late 2026, and Google emails the account administrator 14 days before an account moves. Google says the ads keep the same positions on Search and Maps, and you still pay per lead, not per click.

Is it Google Guaranteed, Google Screened or Google Verified now?

The current badge is Google Verified. It replaced the older badges, including Google Guaranteed and Google Screened. Google's Google Verified page now says it is "simplifying our advertiser badging by launching a single badge for all advertisers," and that it is discontinuing the Money Back Guarantee that came with the Google Guarantee badge. Consumers could request reimbursement for services booked before December 7, 2025, within 30 days of the service being completed. Google says the change does not affect how Local Services Ads rank.

If your truck wrap, quote sheet or website still promises that Google backs your work, it describes a guarantee Google no longer offers. Remove the wording wherever it appears.

How do you get Google Verified as a plumber, electrician or HVAC contractor?

The badge comes from passing Google's screening and verification checks. Google's screening page says the process "varies by business category and location," so your dashboard is the final word on what you owe. In the United States the checks can include the following.

Background checks

Where Google requires them, every owner completes an identity and criminal history check, and so does every employee, subcontractor or other worker who performs services at customers' homes or properties. Office staff who never serve customers are exempt. The business itself gets a check of civil litigation history, such as judgments and liens. Google says there is no credit pull and no charge to the business. Google's background check partners run the checks and answer questions about results.

If a business fails and the decision is final, it must wait 30 days to reapply, and 180 days after a second denial. Those waits are the reason to find out what is on your crew's records before you submit the roster.

Business registration and representative checks

Google verifies U.S. registration with a D-U-N-S number from Dun & Bradstreet. Without one, you can submit Secretary of State registration, an IRS EIN document or state revenue registration. A new D-U-N-S number can take up to 30 days. The person setting up the account must also prove affiliation with the business and complete an identity check with Evident, a Google partner, using a government-issued photo ID.

License checks

Google says it verifies applicable state-level licenses "to the extent possible" against state databases and shows verified licenses on your profile. County and city licenses are on you: you must confirm you hold them and that every service professional is properly licensed. See why that number belongs on your own site in your contractor license number on your website.

Insurance checks

Where a category requires insurance, you upload a certificate of insurance. Google's rules: valid for at least 14 days after submission, a visible policy number, coverage at or above the amount shown on your upload screen, and a name that matches the name you advertise under. If the policy is under a different name, attach your DBA.

Business Profile match

If a Google Business Profile exists for your company, the person doing the checks must be an owner or manager of it. Google says this step can take up to 24 hours to show as done.

Some U.S. advertisers get "accelerated verification": the badge quickly, then 14 days to finish the rest. Google re-evaluates advertisers over time, and skipping a requested review can cost you the badge, pause your ads or get the account suspended. Google's migration notice says verified advertisers moved into Google Ads keep the badge automatically and no longer need insurance and license reverification, though Google may still ask for other business checks.

What does a Local Services lead cost?

Google does not publish a price. Its leads page says lead prices "may vary depending on your location, the job type, the type of lead, or your bidding mode," and that message leads are typically priced lower than phone leads. We found no Google-published average cost per lead for these trades, so we do not quote one.

What Google does publish is how charges work. You are charged for valid leads: a call you answer, a voicemail, a message, a booking request, or a missed call you return. After migration, Google says monthly spend will not exceed your daily average budget multiplied by 30.4, and manual cost-per-lead bidding is gone.

Credits are narrower than many contractors assume. Google's lead credit page says its models reassess charged leads and may credit low-quality ones, usually within 30 days. You can also rate a lead through the feedback survey attached to it in your leads inbox. Google says it uses those ratings to send you better-matched leads and "may also occasionally credit leads you report as poor quality," but there is no formal dispute process. Google also says it "no longer supports credits" for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" leads. Google lists leads it will not credit, including calls outside your business hours, customers researching prices and customers who never answer your callback.

With credits this narrow, most of your cost control happens in the settings, before the phone rings. If you do not do tankless water heater installs or do not drive to the next county, remove that job type or area before you pay for the call, and set business hours that match when someone will actually pick up.

How do reviews work in Local Services Ads?

They come from your Google Business Profile. Google's reviews page says customer reviews are managed through Business Profile, not the ad account, and are governed by Google Maps content policies. Star rating and review count affect how your ad ranks.

Google's ranking page lists the rest: your bid, your responsiveness (missed calls can hurt it), how relevant you are to the search, and profile quality, which includes rating, review count, response time, photos and completed verification checks. Google says higher-quality profiles may rank higher and may pay lower costs per lead, which makes a fast answer on the phone worth money.

How are Local Services Ads different from Google Ads and from your Business Profile?

The difference is in what you pay for and what you control. With Local Services Ads you pay per valid lead, and there are no keywords: you pick service categories, job types and areas, and the ad opens your Local Services profile, a call or a message. With standard Google Ads you generally pay per click, choose keywords and send the click to a page you pick, usually your website.

A Google Business Profile is free and is not an ad at all. It ranks on relevance, distance and prominence, and it links customers to call you or visit your website.

Google's migration notice makes the per-click contrast itself: standard Performance Max campaigns charge "whenever a user clicks your ad," while pay-per-lead campaigns charge only for valid leads. Google's local ranking page says you cannot pay for a better organic Business Profile ranking. For that side, see our Google Business Profile guide.

Does your website still matter if you run Local Services Ads?

Yes, because the ad is not the only thing a homeowner checks.

A homeowner who sees your ad may search your company name before calling. What comes up is your Business Profile, your reviews and your website. Google's Business Profile guidelines expect your name to match the one on your signage, website and stationery, and Google can read category information from your website. A site that shows the same name, license number, service area and services as your verified profile backs up what the badge says, which a thin template page with a stock photo of a van cannot do.

The site also earns calls you never pay for. When your monthly Local Services budget runs out, Google stops showing the ad, but your organic listing keeps working. Our guide to local SEO for plumbers, electricians and HVAC covers how to earn those calls.

What should you have ready before you sign up?

Gather documents first. Google says names that match exactly across documents help avoid delays.

  • Legal business name, DBA and the exact name you will advertise under
  • D-U-N-S number, or Secretary of State, EIN or state revenue registration
  • Government-issued photo ID for the person setting up the account
  • State trade license numbers, plus county and city licenses where required
  • Certificate of insurance with policy number, valid at least 14 more days
  • Roster of every owner and field worker who will need a background check
  • Owner or manager access to your Google Business Profile
  • Accurate service areas, job types and business hours
  • Someone who answers the phone and returns messages fast
  • A website that matches the profile on name, license, area and services

Then set a starting budget you could live with even if every lead in a slow week turned out to be a price shopper.

Common questions about Local Services Ads for contractors

Is the Google Guarantee still available for contractors?

No. Google replaced its badges with a single Google Verified badge for all Local Services advertisers and discontinued the Money Back Guarantee that came with the Google Guarantee badge. Consumers could request reimbursement for services booked before December 7, 2025. Eligible existing advertisers who had completed verification received the new badge automatically.

How long does it take to get Google Verified?

Google does not publish one timeline. Some pieces have their own clocks: Google says a new D-U-N-S number from Dun & Bradstreet can take up to 30 days, the Business Profile check can take up to 24 hours to show as done, and businesses offered accelerated verification have 14 days to finish the remaining steps.

Do I pay for Local Services leads I cannot use?

Sometimes. Google charges for valid leads and may credit leads its models later judge to be low quality, usually within 30 days. You can also report a poor lead through the feedback survey for that lead, and Google says it may occasionally credit those. It lists examples it will not credit, including calls outside your business hours, price shoppers and customers who do not answer your callback, and it no longer credits leads for jobs or areas you do not serve.

Do Local Services Ads use keywords?

No. Google says the campaigns are keywordless. You choose service categories, job types and service areas, and Google matches your profile to searches for those services in those areas.

Can I pause Local Services Ads when my crews are booked?

Yes. Google says you can pause the campaign at any time to stop receiving leads and enable it again later. Pausing is better than letting calls go unanswered, because missed calls can count against your responsiveness, which Google uses in ranking.
